## Stakeholder Framework

To really understand the societal impact and feasibility of EMPOWER, we deliberately strived to think beyond when deciding whom to include as stakeholders. We sought out experts in synthetic biology who could fill the gaps in our knowledge. But beyond the technicalities, it was particularly important to us to understand why female-specific conditions such as PMOS are consistently overlooked in research and funding. We wanted to shed light on the societal and personal consequences of this funding gap carries, and how this situation could be improved by speaking to various representatives of the public healthcare system.

We grouped our stakeholders into five categories: healthcare professionals, scientific & technical experts, biotech entrepreneurs & industry, patients, and advocates & legislators. Within each category, we aimed to reach a diverse group of people to gather as many different perspectives and insights as possible.

<StakeholderGroup title="Scientific & Technical Experts" icon="science">

To learn from their experience, we reached out to knowledgeable scientists, PIs, professors from academia and the industry who have previously worked on projects similar to ours. We knew from the start that introducing an engineered bacterium into an environment as complex as the gut was going to be challenging. We therefore sought out scientists who could provide us with the know-how on the technical aspects, methodological skills and also the current scientific landscape. In total, we consulted [x] people from the synthetic biology field, who helped strengthen our project on a technical level.

</StakeholderGroup>

<StakeholderGroup title="Biotech Entrepreneurs & Industry" icon="industry">

This stakeholder group consists of people with industry experience, ranging from CEOs and advisors to R&D specialists. We reached out to them to gain a market perspective and to prepare our product for potential industrial implementation. In addition, they also gave us feedback on pitching, business development and securing funding to continue the project.

</StakeholderGroup>

<StakeholderGroup title="Patients" icon="patients">

From the very beginning of EMPOWER, we knew that involving the people affected by PMOS would be essential to our project's success. We therefore spoke to [x] women from different countries, recruited through online channels such as Instagram and community group chats, about how living with PMOS influences their daily life. To broaden our pool as much as possible, we included participants with different phenotypes, age groups and countries of origin.

</StakeholderGroup>

<StakeholderGroup title="Advocates & Legislators" icon="legislation">

To highlight the lack of funding that PMOS and other female-specific conditions receive, we contacted representatives, legislators and community advocates.
