Communicating EMPOWER is a two-way street. At every iGEM meetup we presented
our project to other teams, judges and advisors, and every time their questions
changed how we explain it. This page collects what we heard and what we took
away. The events themselves are described on the <Link to="/events">Events</Link>
page.

## Feedback from iGEM meetups

Pick a meetup to see what we were asked, the advice we received, and how we
responded.

<SegmentedSelector>

<SectionItem label="BFH, Hamburg">

**Questions and suggestions from the judges**

- A judge suggested writing a protocol for our kit, so future iGEM teams could
  adapt it to other bacteria.
- Would we use native bacterial parts?
- What are our next steps after iGEM, and which prizes do we aim for?

**How we responded**

- We explained our plan to develop *P. vulgatus*-specific promoters.
- We discussed future mouse studies if our approach works, or improving our
  design if it does not.

**What we took away**

- Use our dry lab work to identify the most conserved BSH gene sequence, and
  explain the link between bile acids, IL-22 and PCOS better.
- We discussed our potential contributions to Human Practices, Measurement and
  Part Collection.
- Every team member must be able to explain our poster, experiments and future
  plans with confidence, so we decided to organise booth shifts for upcoming
  events.

</SectionItem>

<SectionItem label="Utrecht">

**Advice**

- Make the presentation more visual, explain *P. vulgatus* more clearly, and
  focus on our solution and strategy.
- Sowmya, a former iGEM judge, encouraged us to prepare for biosafety
  questions and to document team responsibilities clearly on our wiki.

**Questions we were asked**

- How does diet affect *P. vulgatus*?
- How would we measure and control its abundance?
- Could administering *E. coli* cause an immune response or lead to its
  clearance?

**How we responded**

- Diet may influence bacterial abundance, and we plan to measure both BSH
  activity and bacterial levels.
- Our approach modifies the patient's own *P. vulgatus* outside the body,
  without administering *E. coli*.

This feedback helped us improve our explanations and showed us which areas
needed more attention.

</SectionItem>

<SectionItem label="Münster">

**Questions we were asked**

- How would we monitor and control the modified *P. vulgatus*? (We discussed
  sequencing and colony counting.)
- Why did we choose our CRISPR system?
- How would we deliver the treatment to patients, and how might it affect the
  gut microbiome?
- Could removing BSH increase bacterial growth, and would the strain be
  cleared, requiring repeated treatment?
- How are patients involved, how do we handle biosafety, and how do we prevent
  our engineered DNA from spreading to other bacteria?

**How we responded**

- We explained our plan to modify the patient's own bacteria outside the body
  and return them without the *E. coli* donor.

The last set of questions, on patient involvement, biosafety and DNA spread,
were the most important ones we took home.

</SectionItem>

<SectionItem label="Málaga">

**Advice**

- We learned about entrepreneurship and met an iGEM Space Coordinator, who
  gave us advice on preparing for the Grand Jamboree.
- Focus on our target customers, our market and what makes our solution
  different.
- Make sure every team member understands the project, and document our work
  early.

**Questions we were asked**

- What is the experimental design for conjugation, especially with anaerobic
  bacteria?
- What is our approach to promoter optimisation?
- How would *P. vulgatus* be administered into the body, and with which
  medication or delivery method?
- How do we plan to perform our halo assay?

These questions showed us which areas still needed development.

</SectionItem>

</SegmentedSelector>
