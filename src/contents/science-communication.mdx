Science communication helped us put a spotlight on PMOS while making the condition easier to understand. We focused our engagement on three audiences: (young) students, patients and the wider public. At a range of events and information booths, we set out to inspire, raise awareness and teach people about iGEM and our project, EMPOWER. Beyond these in-person activities, we shared our journey on Instagram and LinkedIn. Additionally, we had the opportunity to publish articles on the ISALA project's blog and in Flair magazine. To support the community in a more lasting way, we also developed an app made for the wider public, not only for people with PMOS. Finally, we collaborated with other iGEM teams to highlight the importance of community within iGEM and to show that science communication can be entertaining too.

<TabSection label="Digital Science Communication">

On Instagram and LinkedIn, we put a spotlight on PMOS. We started with the basics: what PMOS is, how it affects the body, and how our project EMPOWER aims to help. From there, our symptom spotlight series explored the symptoms one by one, and we shared where to find help and further resources. Based on the recommendations we received from <Link to="/human-practices#sarah-noelmans">Sarah Noelmans</Link>, we highlighted the role of diet in managing PMOS by sharing a PMOS-friendly meal. We also raised awareness of the striking funding gap in PMOS research, showing how little attention the condition receives compared to how many people it affects.

The ISALA project is a citizen science project at the University of Antwerp that studies the vaginal microbiome and its role in women's health. It is coordinated by <Link to="/human-practices#sarah-lebeer">Prof. Sarah Lebeer</Link>. Both of our projects share the same mission: bringing microbes and reproductive health out of the shadows. Through this collaboration, we wanted to reach ISALA's large and engaged community to draw attention to PMOS and our project. In their blog post, we introduced PMOS and presented our <Link to="/description">goals</Link>.

We also had the opportunity to publish an article in Flair, a popular Belgian women's magazine covering lifestyle, fashion and health. We answered the most common questions about PMOS, from what the condition is to why its name has changed. Our goal was to reach a broad audience of young women outside the scientific world, to bring awareness and education to those who may recognize the symptoms in themselves without knowing the name.

<TabSection label="App">

During our patient interviews, most women told us they felt they did not have enough educational resources provided by their doctor. In addition, they felt like they had to really get to know their body on their own by tracking nutrition, lifestyle, symptoms and treatment. To support all women beyond our engagement, posts and articles, we created an app that brings everything they need together in one place. The app is made for the wider public, not just for PMOS patients. A built-in personalized period tracker helps users follow their cycle and spot irregularities. Alongside it, the app offers practical tips for daily life, short courses that explain PMOS step by step, and a collection of trusted resources for further support. Users can also learn about EMPOWER and how our research aims to improve the lives of people with PMOS in the future. Check out the app below.
