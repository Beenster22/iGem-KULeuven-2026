**“Science is not finished until it’s communicated.” - Sir Mark Walport**

---

As part of the internet generation, we have witnessed how misinformation can spread rapidly online, deepening the divide between the scientific community and the general public. This gap has always existed but feels more urgent in today’s fast-paced digital environment. For instance, the data showing that the measles, mumps and rabies (MMR) vaccine may correlate with autism stem from one singular publication, which was recently redacted from the journal for being “incorrect [and] contrary to findings of an earlier investigation” 1.  This is why one of our central priorities has been to communicate our project, our values and our failures openly and honestly, helping to bridge that gap. The events we attended throughout the year made it possible to put this into practice. From Job fairs to educational festivals, we had the pleasure of connecting with many different groups of people: young children, industry professionals, curious members of the public, and like-minded scientists. Through these educational and outreach efforts, we hope to inspire and inform the public on topics like women’s health and the importance of science in our lives.
