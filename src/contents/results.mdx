## Antibiotic susceptibility (MIC)

### Introduction

The MIC assay is part of the toolkit development, which has the aim of facilitating research with
_Phocaeicola vulgatus_. As _P. vulgatus_ is not a model organism, not as much is known about media optimal
for growth, most effective antibiotics, compatible fluorescent reporters etc. on a strain level. The goal of
the MIC assay is to determine which antibiotics are most effective at inhibiting growth of _P. vulgatus_
DSM1447/ATCC8482 and fecal strain _P. vulgatus_ RCC1806. An application in the therapeutics pipeline is to use
the most effective antibiotic to inhibit _P. vulgatus_, transformed with the CRISPR construct, growth in the
case of uncontrolled growth.

### Materials and methods

_P. vulgatus_ DSM1447 was obtained from the research group Molecular Biotechnology of Plants and
Micro-organisms from KU Leuven by Matt Herdman. The clinical isolate _P. vulgatus_ RCC1806 was obtained from
the Raes lab of Bioinformatics and (Eco-)Systems Biology by Ruben Vazquez Uribe. The antibiotics ampicillin,
chloramphenicol, erythromycin, metronidazole, kanamycin, spectinomycin, tetracycline, and gentamicin were
used, as well as statin (simvastatin). Simvastatin is not an antibiotic, but has been reported to inhibit the
growth of _P. vulgatus_ DSM1447[^mic-escalante].

Working concentrations in a two-fold serial dilution for the initial MIC assays were taken from literature.
Antibiotics were diluted in mGAM. The experiment was carried out anaerobically and in triplicate. It was
repeated if the MIC was not in the concentration range of the antibiotic or if the replicates did not give a
consensus. The percentage of inhibition was calculated:

<div className="text-center my-3">
  % inhibition = 1 − (OD<sub>well</sub> − OD<sub>blank</sub>) / (OD<sub>positive control</sub> − OD<sub>blank</sub>)
</div>

<div className="figure-caption">
  Equation 1. The percentage of inhibition used to determine the MIC of a replicate of a strain for an
  antibiotic. The blank was the inoculated antibiotic solvent, e.g. 50% ethanol for ampicillin. The positive
  control was inoculated mGAM.
</div>

The MIC of a repeat was the lowest antibiotic concentration that inhibits visible growth. The
IC<sub>90%</sub>, the minimal concentration needed to result in 90% growth inhibition, was used to provide
quantitative data[^mic-veiga]. The OD630 of each well was measured after incubating for 48h at 30°C. The
IC<sub>90%</sub> of a strain for an antibiotic was the consensus of the IC<sub>90%</sub> of the repeats. If
there were two concentrations at which the same number of repeats indicated a IC<sub>90%</sub>, e.g. three
repeats indicate a IC<sub>90%</sub> = 2 µg/mL and three repeats indicate a IC<sub>90%</sub> = 4 µg/mL, the
highest value was taken as the IC<sub>90%</sub>.

### Results

The antibiotic with the lowest IC<sub>90%</sub> is the most effective against _P. vulgatus_ and can be used in
the therapeutics pipeline to inhibit growth if needed. Tetracycline was found to be the most effective
antibiotic, followed by chloramphenicol and metronidazole (Figure 1). Ampicillin and erythromycin showed more
variable results between the two strains. Gentamicin and kanamycin did not inhibit _P. vulgatus_ within the
tested range, except for RCC1806, which had a IC<sub>90%</sub> of 256 mg/L for gentamicin.

While tetracycline had the lowest IC<sub>90%</sub>, the range was several orders of two high for
_P. vulgatus_ DSM1447, and there was only one replicate for _P. vulgatus_ RCC1806. To justify tetracycline as
the most effective antibiotic (although insignificant compared to ampicillin, chloramphenicol, erythromycin,
and metronidazole) for _P. vulgatus_, the same results, but now with an inhibition cutoff of 85%, are included
(Figure 2). In some dilutions, wells with a lower antibiotic concentration had inhibition values of above 90%,
while wells with the highest antibiotic concentration in a dilution sometimes had values of 88% or 89%. As a
result, these replicates, including the replicates of tetracycline, were discarded when using a cutoff of 90%.
Thus, when lowering the cutoff to 85%, a more nuanced figure appears, which justifies the choice of
tetracycline, metronidazole, and chloramphenicol as the most effective antibiotics for both DSM1447 and
RCC1806.

Ampicillin is a β-lactam antibiotic, and β-lactamases have been reported to be present in _P. vulgatus_, so
resistance was expected[^mic-mahmood][^mic-parker][^mic-sabunwala]. In contrast, the MIC of DSM1447 is 4 mg/L
(1-32 mg/L), and the MIC of RCC1806 is 64 mg/L. Similar results have been reported (Table 1). Possibly,
β-lactamases are not present or not as actively expressed in these strains than in the resistant clinical
isolates. EUCAST advises not to use ampicillin for anaerobic bacteria when the MIC is higher than
0.5 mg/L[^mic-eucast-nobp]. This advice, combined with the variability in IC<sub>90%</sub> between DSM1447 and
RCC1806, means that ampicillin will no longer be considered as an effective antibiotic against _P. vulgatus_
DSM1447 and RCC1806.

For both strains the IC<sub>90%</sub> of metronidazole is at the breakpoint of 4 mg/L, indicating that
metronidazole is most likely an effective therapeutical antibiotic[^mic-eucast-bp]. A recent meta-analysis
confirms resistance against metronidazole remains low[^mic-markowska]. No breakpoints or other advice
concerning tetracycline, chloramphenicol, or erythromycin are available for _P. vulgatus_.

Gentamycin and kanamycin uptake are dependent on the proton motive force, and so indirectly on oxygen, and
were thus not predicted to have an adverse effect on growth[^mic-krause][^mic-webster], and this is indeed
confirmed by our results.

### Conclusion

Tetracycline, chloramphenicol, and metronidazole were found to be the most effective antibiotics against
_P. vulgatus_ DSM1447 and RCC1806. As the EUCAST specifically recommends the use of metronidazole when the MIC
is below 4 mg/L, this antibiotic is most recommended when working with _P. vulgatus_. Ampicillin and
erythromycin are less encouraged, given the variability between the strains and a negative advice against
ampicillin by EUCAST.
