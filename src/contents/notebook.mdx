**Goal.** Determine which *P. vulgatus* strains the project would be valuable to include in the further downstream project.

**Hypothesis.** Based on the work of García-López M, the most valuable strain to use would be the type strain DSMZ 1447/ ATCC 8482. Thanks to the Raes lab and our PI, Ruben Vazquez Uribe, we were able to work with a fecal derived strain which could give us more insight in human-derived *P. vuglatus* strains. Other strains described in literature could be useful for downstream use to compare different experimental processes and techniques.

**Method.** Two strains were taken to research in more detail from the start: the type strain *P. vulgatus* DSM1447/ ATCC8482 (‘1447’) and the fecal isolate RC1806 (‘1806’). For the later, colonies from an existing plate provided by the Raes lab, were streaked onto WC and mGAM plates and inoculated into broth to confirm viability.

**Materials used:** Cryostocks of 1447 and plated 1806; WC and mGAM plates and liquid broth.

- 2026-07-24: mGAM cryostock transferred to WC plates for both 1447 and 1806 and grown over the weekend.

- 2026-07-27:  the weekend WC plates gave less colony growth than the mGAM plates for both strains.

**Goal.** Determine which of four media (mGAM, BHI, WC and minimal medium (MM)) best supports growth of *P. vulgatus* 1447.

**Hypothesis.** Based on literature, mGAM and supplemented BHI are expected to result in the highest growth and best vitality of the bacteria. As Minimal Medium only contains the most necessary components provided for *P. vuglatus*, it is expected that this medium would result in the lowest amount of countable colonies.

**Method.** Overnight cultures from the mGAM cryostock in each of the four media, OD600 read against the sterile medium as blank. A 96-well plate was then laid out with 100 µL dH₂O in the edge wells, 100 µL of each sterile medium as a sterility control, and the overnight cultures diluted 10 µL into 1 mL of the matching medium. The plate was sealed and read in the EPOCH2 plate reader.

**Protocol:** *(to be added)*

**Materials used.** mGAM-preserved cryostock of 1447, mGAM, unsupplemented BHI, WC and MM broth, EPOCH2 plate reader.

Based on further observations throughout the project, we could conclude that *P. vulgatus* in mGAM grew the fastest and resulted each time in the same high OD600 values.

This result was rather expected and shows already the importance of different components in the medium. This is researched further in STR-03.

**Discussion.** Discussion with Matt on 2026-07-31 explained both failures and backed up our previous predictions. Unsupplemented BHI does not contain hematin or vitamin K, both of which *P. vulgatus* needs, which accounts for the flat BHI curves. The old-BHI plate test is consistent with this. Separately, the 1:100 dilution used throughout was far too high; Matt recommended at least 1:10,000, otherwise debris and dead cells dominate the reading. A longer lag phase is normal for this organism, but 24 h is unexpectedly long and had no ready explanation at the time; it was later traced to preculture state. Matt also suggested that where cells are needed in exponential phase, a dilution series grown overnight gives a range of OD600 to pick from.

**Next step.** Abandon the four-medium comparison and test supplementation of MM against mGAM in a factorial design: STR-03.

**Safety.** As *P. vulgatus* is an anaerobic bacterium and an organism categorized as BSL 2, we worked in a sterile anaerobic chamber. Handling accordingly to the biosafety level and the required regulations.

**Goal.** Find a gene present in *P. vulgatus* but absent from *E. coli*, so that colonies growing on the selective plates can be confirmed as transconjugants rather than surviving donor.

**Hypothesis.** ParB is a suitable marker. It encodes a DNA-binding protein involved in chromosome segregation and partitioning at cell division. Wild-type and laboratory strains of *E. coli* organise and segregate their chromosome through other systems such as MukBEF rather than a chromosomal parA–parB–parS system, so the gene should be present in *P. vulgatus* and absent from *E. coli*. **Method.** The ParB sequence was searched against the genomes of 1447 and *E. coli* DH5α. Different primers were then designed in SnapGene and ordered from IDT. Multiple combinations were tested of forward and reverse primers to make sure a correct binding set was found.

<NotebookSection title="PRM-02 - Promoter and RBS library design">

<NotebookEntryMeta
  dates="2026-08-01 to 2026-08-21"
  people="DS, OJ, MG, AL, with Ruben"
  status="Done: constructs ordered"
  buildsOn="/"
  followedBy="PRM-04"
/>

**Goal.** Assemble a set of promoters to characterise in *P. vulgatus*, and decide whether to vary the RBS alongside them.

**Hypothesis.** Mutating a promoter while leaving its −33 and −7 boxes intact will shift its strength without removing its activity completely. Changing those boxes breaks the promoter. Varying the RBS alongside the promoter would make the output difficult to compare, since RBS changes produce a clear drop in activity on their own.

**Method.** Literature search for described *Bacteroides* promoters, with sequences pulled from supplementary data into a spreadsheet. Separately, a mutation library built computationally from the PBT1311 wild-type sequence: a script that preserves the −33 and −7 boxes and introduces 13 random mutations elsewhere, generating 20 variants.

**Materials used.** Published promoter sequences; PBT1311_Mutation13_library.txt.

**In-lab notes.**

- 2026-08-01: 25 promoters collected from the literature, including four inducible promoters and one housekeeping promoter, PBT1311 from *B. theta*, which was the most widely described. In several papers the promoter and RBS were reported fused; these were separated, which was difficult as the region is not well conserved, but largely succeeded.

- 2026-08-01: 13 mutations chosen for the variant library: out of roughly 80 nucleotides this sits just under 15%, judged high enough to shift strength and low enough not to break the sequence.

- 2026-08-01: 13 RBSs collected, four of them separated out of fused inducible-promoter sequences. Some are weak by the authors' own account.

- 2026-08-18: primers arrived from IDT.

- 2026-08-21: promoter constructs (gBLOCKS) ordered.

**Results.** 45 promoters in total: 25 from the literature plus 20 computational variants of PBT1311. Thirteen RBSs catalogued but not taken forward. Four promoters selected for the screen, appearing in the constructs as Bfp, BT, PBT and Cfx.

**Reporter choice.** Ruben's guidance on 2026-07-08 was that iLOV does not work well for weak promoters, being too dim, while GFP and mCherry are good choices because both mature in the presence of oxygen. That guidance reads differently in hindsight: the constructs that used KOFP-7 are the ones most exposed to the detection problem found in <EntryLink to="CNJ-07" />, where no fluorescence could be seen at all.

|  | Members |
| --- | --- |
| Promotors (constitutive) | Bfp1E6, BT1311, PBT4001, cfxA, PAM3 |
| Reporters | GFP, KOFP7, mCherry, Nanoluc |

**Conclusion.** The screen is a promoter library, not a promoter × RBS library.

**Discussion.** The decision to drop the RBS arm was made with Ruben on 2026-08-14, and had been the direction from the start: the briefing on 2026-07-08 already set out varying one promoter and holding the RBS fixed, with the Anderson promoter library based on σ70 as the literature starting point. Most of the literature refers to one specific RBS, and changing it produces a decrease in activity.

**Next step.** Amplify the pNBU2 backbone for Gibson assembly: PRM-04.

**Safety.** BSL 1, in silico.

<ProtocolNote kind="important">

Why gel extraction rather than a straight clean-up. The point is to remove the original pNBU2 template. Without it (or with only a partial clean-up such as DpnI treatment) transformation gives a mixture of colonies, some carrying the newly cloned vector and some the original. For a single construct that would just mean more colony PCR. With roughly 16 promoters being cloned at once it becomes unmanageable, so gel extraction is what makes the downstream screening tractable: whatever grows on the plate should carry a new vector.

</ProtocolNote>
